Proposal

Last-mile digital skills, with the evidence built in

I take digital skills and EdTech programmes into the places most providers will not go, across pastoralist, rural and underserved East Africa, and I come back with evidence a funder will accept. The measurement is designed in the same pass as the curriculum, so the proof arrives with the programme instead of being reconstructed at reporting time.

The problem I am usually called about

Most digital skills programmes can tell you how many people attended. Far fewer can tell you what changed. The gap is rarely effort. It is that measurement gets bolted on at reporting time, by which point the data that would have proved the outcome was never collected. A second version of the same problem: the programme works while the person who built it is in the room, and stalls when they leave.

Who this is for

A good fit

  • Funders and INGOs holding a grant commitment in a hard-to-reach region, without a delivery partner who can evidence the outcome.
  • Programme leads who inherited something that produces activity reports and cannot answer what actually changed.
  • EdTech builders whose product has to survive shared devices, thin bandwidth and unreliable power before it survives anything else.
  • Organisations holding learner records they have not yet brought in line with Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019.
  • Teams that need the programme to keep running after the consultant leaves.

A poor fit

  • Urban, well-connected settings where plenty of capable providers already operate. This is not where I am useful.
  • A single workshop with no measurement attached to it.
  • Work where the deliverable is headcount trained. I can produce that number, but it is the one I trust least.
  • A software build with no programme wrapped around it.
  • Evidence assembled after the fact to support a conclusion already written.

How an engagement runs

01

Diagnostic

What the programme claims, what it can currently evidence, and the distance between the two. Ends with a written read on whether the thing is working.

02

Design

Curriculum, cohort architecture, selection and delivery model, with the LTP and SEL frameworks underneath. The KPI framework is written here, not later.

03

Stand-up

Recruitment and selection, onboarding, trainer enablement, and running the first cohort through to completion with me accountable for it.

04

Evidence

Salesforce and Power BI pipelines, learner records kept to the Data Protection Act, and the donor reporting that comes out of them rather than around them.

05

Handover

Standard operating procedures, Trainer of Trainers, and curricula another trainer can pick up cold. The test is what still runs after I leave.

06

Advisory seat

For teams who have delivery covered and want the design and the measurement pressure-tested at intervals rather than built from scratch.

The conditions I design for

This is the part that narrows the field. These are not edge cases to plan around. They are the normal operating environment, and a programme that has not been designed for them will not survive its first term.

  • Power that goes out, and sessions that have to continue anyway
  • Connectivity that is intermittent, metered, or absent for days
  • Shared and donated devices, with no guarantee of the same machine twice
  • Learners who have never switched on a computer
  • Field sites reached on rural transport, scheduled around school calendars, exams and the long rains
  • Learner records that carry real safeguarding and data-protection obligations

What you would be buying

One person who has done all three jobs this work needs, rather than three vendors who each do one. I have run the programmes end to end, built the Salesforce and Power BI systems that report on them, and hold current certification in both AI literacy, through the Raspberry Pi Foundation and Google DeepMind, and cybersecurity, through AfricaHackon Academy. The delivery, the measurement and the digital safety are designed together because the same person is doing them.
